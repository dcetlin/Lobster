§1 How TikTok Actually Works

Understanding the platform before touching the ad tools

§1.1 TikTok is a content distribution engine, not a social network mental model

The most important thing to understand about TikTok is what it rewards: watch time and completion rate, not follower count or social proximity. Unlike Instagram, where you're primarily surfaced to your existing followers, TikTok's algorithm serves any video to any user if early engagement signals look strong.

What this means practically:

  • A Jewels Jar post with zero followers can reach 50,000 people if the first 100 viewers watch it to the end
  • An account with 100,000 followers will get suppressed distribution if its videos are swiped past in the first two seconds
  • The paid ads layer sits on top of this same organic distribution system — Spark Ads (boosted organic posts) benefit from the organic performance signal directly
TikTok First Principle

Every piece of content you put money behind should first pass the question: would a stranger watch this for 15 seconds? If you can't answer yes with confidence, the ad budget will be wasted. The algorithm reads completion signals in the first hour. A weak-performing post will stay weak regardless of spend.

§1.2 The three signals TikTok's algorithm weighs core mechanics

The TikTok algorithm distributes content based on three layers of signal, in rough order of weight:

  1. Video completion rate — What percentage of viewers watched to the end? This is the primary signal. A video that gets rewatched (replay rate) is even stronger.
  2. Engagement actions — Likes, comments, shares, saves. Shares carry more weight than likes because they signal the viewer found the content valuable enough to send to someone else.
  3. Audience fit signal — Did the people who engaged match the target? TikTok's system learns what type of viewer responds well and refines distribution toward more of them.

What TikTok does not weight heavily: follower count (for organic distribution), post timing, or hashtag quantity. These are common obsessions that don't move the needle nearly as much as completion rate.

§1.3 Sound-on, full-screen, entertainment-first environment creative context

The TikTok feed is categorically different from Instagram:

  • Full-screen vertical — the video occupies the entire phone display. There is no sidebar, no caption initially visible, no profile context visible. The video has to do everything.
  • Sound-on by default — unlike Instagram where most users scroll with sound off, TikTok users have sound on by default. Music, voiceover, and sound design are creative elements, not optional.
  • Entertainment first — users come to be entertained and discover, not to shop. An ad that looks like a commercial creates friction. Content that feels like organic creation does not.
  • Fast swipe culture — if the first 1–2 seconds don't create a reason to keep watching, the viewer swipes. This is the hook problem.

§2.4 Smart+ Campaigns vs Manual — the automation question defer until later

Smart+ is TikTok's AI-managed campaign layer — similar to Meta's Advantage+. You hand control of targeting, bidding, and audience selection to TikTok's algorithm, and it optimizes toward your objective.

When Smart+ makes sense:

  • Pixel has accumulated purchase event history (100+ events)
  • You have 5+ creative assets to feed it
  • You're spending $100+/day and want to reduce management overhead

Why to start manually: At campaign launch, manual setup forces you to understand what the platform variables are doing. Smart+ is a black box — when it works, great; when it underperforms, there's no diagnostic lever to pull. Manual campaigns tell you what audience, what placement, what creative is working. That knowledge is the foundation Smart+ needs anyway.

Defer Smart+ until you have a campaign that's profitable manually. Then let Smart+ try to beat it.

§2.5 TikTok Pixel — what it is and why it's required technical prerequisite

The TikTok Pixel is a piece of JavaScript code installed on the Jewels Jar website. It fires events back to TikTok when visitors take specific actions, allowing TikTok to:

  1. Attribute purchases to ads — which ad did this buyer see before purchasing?
  2. Optimize delivery — show the ad to more people who look like buyers
  3. Build retargeting audiences — create an audience of people who visited the site but didn't buy

Key events to fire:

  • PageView — any page visited
  • ViewContent — product page viewed
  • AddToCart — item added to cart
  • InitiateCheckout — checkout started
  • CompletePayment — purchase completed (TikTok's equivalent of "Purchase")
Shopify setup path

TikTok has an official Shopify Sales Channel app that installs the Pixel and connects the product catalog in a few clicks. This is the fastest path to Pixel parity. After installation, verify events are firing in TikTok's Events Manager before spending any money.

§2.6 TikTok Shop — the in-app commerce layer evaluate

TikTok Shop allows US users to purchase directly within the TikTok app — the product, checkout, and payment happen without leaving TikTok. For sellers, this is a separate storefront from the Shopify site, with its own product catalog and fulfillment logistics.

Why it matters for Jewels Jar:

  • Removes the friction of "tap the link in bio → go to website → add to cart → checkout" — each step loses buyers
  • TikTok Shop products appear organically in the TikTok feed when creators tag them — it's discoverability without ads
  • Shop Ads (a TikTok ad format specifically for Shop products) have lower CPCs than standard In-Feed ads in the current early-market period

Trade-offs to evaluate:

  • TikTok Shop takes a commission (currently 6–8% in the US)
  • Shopify integration exists but requires setup and SKU management across two catalogs
  • US shipping logistics complexity is not resolved by TikTok Shop by itself

TikTok Shop is not a prerequisite for running TikTok ads. It's an additional channel to evaluate. Don't let evaluating it block running standard campaigns.

§3 Creative Framework

Making videos that TikTok and viewers both reward

§3.1 The hook problem — the first 2 seconds decide everything creative core

The TikTok hook is the single most leveraged creative decision. The algorithm reads the percentage of viewers who watched past the 3-second mark (called thumb-stop rate or hook rate) as a primary signal. A video with a strong hook gets pushed to more people. A video where 60% of viewers swipe in the first 2 seconds gets suppressed.

Hook patterns that work for jewelry:

  • Visual reveal hook — Start close-up on something else, then jewelry comes into frame dramatically. Or open with the jewelry already in motion (swinging earring, spinning ring in someone's hand).
  • Contrast hook — "I never thought I'd spend money on jewelry until I tried this." Creates immediate narrative tension.
  • Reaction hook — Someone's genuine reaction to seeing or unwrapping a piece. Authenticity reads well.
  • Question hook — "What do you wear when you want to feel put-together without trying?" Then the video answers it.
  • The stack/collection hook — Lay out several pieces together in an aesthetically arranged flat lay or "jewelry unboxing" format. TikTok jewelry content that shows volume of pieces in a satisfying visual arrangement performs well.

Hooks that don't work on TikTok: Starting with the brand name or logo. Starting with a slow product shot on a white background. Starting with a talking head intro ("Hi, I'm Tania from…"). None of these create a reason to keep watching within 2 seconds.

§3.2 The three-part TikTok ad structure creative formula

Most high-performing TikTok ads (especially for physical products) follow a three-part structure, even if it's not explicit:

  1. Hook (0–2 sec) — Create a reason to keep watching. Visual interest, pattern interrupt, or emotional trigger. The viewer should not yet know it's an ad.
  2. Value delivery (3–12 sec) — Show the product doing its thing. For jewelry: on the body, in motion, in context. Include any key message (quality, story, occasion). This is where you earn the click.
  3. CTA (last 2–3 sec) — A clear, specific instruction. "Shop the link below," "Tap to shop," "Get yours now." Don't be subtle here. Viewers who've watched to this point are warm.

Total length: 15–30 seconds is the sweet spot for jewelry. Under 15 seconds can work for simple product showcases. Over 30 seconds requires the hook and value delivery to both be strong enough to sustain attention.

The sound layer

Add music or voiceover. A voiceover that mirrors what the viewer is visually seeing doubles the sensory reinforcement. Trending sounds on TikTok can organically boost distribution — if a sound is trending and the content fits, using it is not gimmicky. TikTok's Creative Center shows trending sounds by region.

§3.3.5 Trending sounds — the TikTok-specific distribution lever TikTok-specific

TikTok's algorithm gives mild organic distribution boosts to content using sounds that are currently trending on the platform. For paid ads, this matters for Spark Ads (which run the organic post including its chosen sound) but not for In-Feed Ads (which overlay their own audio). The mechanic is worth understanding:

  • How to find trending sounds: TikTok's Creative Center (ads.tiktok.com/creative-center) has a "Trending" tab showing sounds by industry and region. Filter for "Beauty & Personal Care" or "Fashion" in the US to find sounds already performing in adjacent categories.
  • The fit test: Only use a trending sound if the content can authentically use it — forced alignment with a trending sound looks artificial and damages the native feel that's the whole point. A sound that fits naturally is a distribution bonus; a sound that's forced is a liability.
  • For jewelry content specifically: aesthetic/moodboard sounds (soft, slightly nostalgic, feminine) tend to fit naturally. High-energy trending sounds often feel jarring against jewelry content. The fit matters more than the trendiness.
  • Original audio: Some TikTok jewelry accounts have built recognizable "audio identities" — a recurring piece of music associated with the brand. This can build recognition over time, though it's a longer-term play.

When briefing Ashley for Spark Ad content, include a suggested sound or sound category — don't leave audio selection entirely to the creator unless they deeply understand the brand's aesthetic register.

§3.5 Creative fatigue — when to refresh and what refresh means ongoing ops

Signs of creative fatigue on TikTok:

  • Thumb-stop rate (3-second view %) declining while budget stays constant
  • CTR declining week-over-week without audience changes
  • ROAS declining without landing page or pricing changes

Refresh cadence at typical starting budget ($50–100/day): Expect to introduce new creative every 4–6 weeks. TikTok creative fatigues faster than Meta because the same audience sees content more repeatedly in a smaller feed environment.

What a creative refresh actually means: Not re-shooting the same content with different lighting. The hook needs to be structurally different. New angle, new pattern interrupt, new first line. The product and CTA can remain consistent — the attention-capture mechanism needs to change.

§4 Campaign Construction

How to build and structure TikTok campaigns

§4.1 Audience targeting on TikTok setup

Targeting types available:

  • Interest targeting — Users who've engaged with content in categories: Jewelry, Fashion, Beauty, Luxury Goods, Accessories. Good starting point with no Pixel history.
  • Behavioral targeting — Users who've taken specific actions on TikTok (liked jewelry videos, interacted with fashion creator content, visited TikTok Shop for accessories). More granular than interest alone.
  • Demographic — Age, gender, location, language. Recommended starting point: US national, women 22–38 for cold prospecting. Let data narrow this over time.
  • Custom audiences — Built from your Pixel data (site visitors, add-to-cart, checkout starts), email/phone list upload, or TikTok engagement (video viewers, followers, profile visitors).
  • Lookalike audiences — TikTok finds users similar to a seed audience. Best seeds: Purchase Pixel events (highest intent), or email customer list. Requires minimum 1,000 seed users for quality lookalike.
Starting targeting recommendation for US launch

For first campaigns: Interest + Behavioral combined (jewelry, fashion, accessories behaviors), US national, women 22–38. This is a cold start — no Pixel history and no customer list yet. This targeting generates the first data pool from which lookalikes and Custom Audiences can be built later.

§4.2 Cold / Warm / Hot audience framework campaign structure

Run separate campaigns for each audience temperature. Don't mix cold and warm audiences in the same ad group — they need different creative and different objectives.

  • Cold (prospecting) — People who don't know Jewels Jar. Interest + behavioral targeting or lookalikes. Creative: hook-first, entertaining, product context. Objective: Traffic or Conversions.
  • Warm (consideration) — Site visitors, video viewers (watched 50%+ of a TikTok), TikTok engagers. Creative: product-forward, reviews, offer specificity. Objective: Conversions.
  • Hot (retargeting) — Add-to-cart, checkout abandoners, past purchasers. Creative: the specific product they looked at, with a push reason (urgency, free shipping reminder, social proof). Objective: Conversions with Purchase event.

Budget allocation for a US launch:

60–70% → Cold
Building the US audience from scratch. This is the primary job of the first 60 days.
20–30% → Warm
Converting people who've been exposed. Pool is small early; grows over time.
10% → Hot
Recovering abandoners. Highest ROAS but smallest audience at launch.
§4.3 Budget thresholds and the learning phase budget decision

TikTok's algorithm requires data to optimize. The "learning phase" is the period where the algorithm is figuring out who to show your ad to. During learning phase, results are noisier and cost is typically higher.

TikTok minimum budgets:

  • Campaign level: $50/day minimum
  • Ad group level: $20/day minimum
  • Per-ad budget (for micro-testing): $20–50/day per creative

To exit the learning phase and get reliable data: TikTok recommends 50 optimization events per ad group per week. For a Conversions campaign optimizing for Purchase, that means 50 purchases/week from that ad group — which requires significant spend at typical CPA rates. For a Traffic campaign optimizing for clicks, the threshold is easier to reach.

Implication: At launch, if purchase CPA is unknown and high, start with a Traffic campaign objective to generate site visit data more affordably. Once you have click-to-purchase conversion rate data from Shopify, you can calculate the budget required to exit learning phase on a Conversions campaign.

Total budget for a meaningful US TikTok test (first 30 days):

  • Minimum to get real signal: $1,500
  • Comfortable learning budget: $2,000–$3,000
  • This is not "optimize for ROAS" spend — it is signal-generation spend. Expecting 3x+ ROAS in the first 30 days sets up disappointment.

§5 Measurement & Reading Performance

The metrics that matter and what they actually mean

§5.1 TikTok-specific metrics and benchmarks measurement
MetricWhat it measuresTarget / Benchmark
Thumb-stop rate
(3-second view rate)		 % of viewers who watched past the first 3 seconds. This is TikTok's primary creative quality signal. Target: 25–30%+. Below 15%: creative hook problem.
Video completion rate % who watched the full video. For a 15-second ad, a higher rate means the hook AND the content delivered value. Target: 15–25% for 15–30 sec ads. Above 30%: exceptional creative.
CPM Cost to reach 1,000 people. TikTok CPMs are typically lower than Meta in the US in 2026. Jewelry category: approximately $8–15. Higher during peak seasons.
CTR (click-through rate) % of impressions that result in a click to the site or shop. Good: 1.5–3%+. Below 0.8%: creative not converting interest to action.
CPC Cost per click. Derived from CPM ÷ CTR. Tells you how much you pay per site visitor. Varies by targeting and creative quality.
CPA Cost per acquisition (per purchase). The north-star conversion metric. Must be below (AOV × gross margin) to be profitable.
ROAS Revenue per dollar of ad spend. Overall campaign profitability signal. Break-even: 1 ÷ gross margin %. Target for scaling: 3–4x. Exceptional: 5x+.
Frequency Average times a unique user has seen the ad. High frequency = audience exhaustion. Pause or refresh creative when frequency exceeds 4–5.

Break-even ROAS formula: 1 ÷ gross margin %. If Jewels Jar's margin is 60%, break-even ROAS = 1.67x. At 3x ROAS, every $1 of ad spend generates $1.33 in margin over break-even. At 5x, it's $2.33. Know your margin before interpreting ROAS.

§5.2 The diagnostic reading order analysis framework

When a campaign is underperforming, diagnose in sequence — don't jump straight to ROAS:

  1. Check Impressions and delivery — Is the ad running? Are bids competitive enough to win placement?
  2. Check Thumb-stop rate — Is the hook working? (below 20% = creative problem, fix the hook)
  3. Check Completion rate — Is the body of the video holding attention? (if hook rate is OK but completion is low, the hook attracted the wrong audience or the content didn't deliver)
  4. Check CTR — Are viewers clicking? (if completion is OK but CTR is low, the CTA is failing or the product isn't compelling enough at this price)
  5. Check Add-to-cart rate in Shopify — Are clickers interested? (low ATC = landing page or product page problem, not the ad)
  6. Check Checkout conversion in Shopify — Are cart-adders completing? (low checkout = friction, often shipping cost)
  7. Finally check ROAS and CPA — Is the full system profitable?

TikTok's attribution and Shopify's attribution will disagree — this is normal. TikTok claims credit for purchases that Shopify may attribute to organic or direct traffic, because of view-through attribution and cross-device behavior. Use 7-day click attribution in TikTok for the most conservative read. Treat a 15–20% discrepancy as expected.

§6.3 US purchase triggers for jewelry — what to say and what frame to use messaging strategy

US TikTok jewelry buyers respond to specific frames. What works:

  • "Everyday jewelry that doesn't look basic" — US buyers, especially 22–35 women, are saturated with mass-market options (Mejuri, Kendra Scott). Distinctiveness-without-effort is a strong motivator.
  • Self-gifting / "treat yourself" frame — Gen Z normalizes self-purchase. "You don't need an occasion" and "this is for you" perform well. The luxury positioning can be present but shouldn't be the lead.
  • Milestone occasions — Birthday, graduation, promotion, anniversary (self or gifting). These have higher AOV intent and convert more readily. "The best birthday gift I've given myself" is a proven hook format.
  • Styling versatility — "This goes with everything" and "I've worn this three days in a row" are authentic, low-friction purchase rationales. Show the piece in multiple contexts (casual outfit, work outfit, elevated outfit).
  • Craft and origin story — There is appetite for "not mass-produced" storytelling on TikTok. "Every piece is [made/sourced/designed by someone with a vision]" resonates if delivered authentically, not as marketing copy.

Frames to avoid or de-emphasize for cold US prospecting: Formal occasion framing (US casual jewelry market is where TikTok buyers live). Price-led messaging ("buy now, sale ends soon") as the primary hook — it signals commodity and undercuts premium positioning.

Competing against Mejuri, Gorjana, Kendra Scott

These brands dominate the $75–200 US jewelry DTC market and will be in the same TikTok interest targeting pool. Jewels Jar cannot out-awareness them — they have years of US Pixel data and larger creative budgets. The competitive position should lean into what they don't offer:

  • Distinctiveness and non-mass identity — Mejuri is beautiful but everyone has it. Jewels Jar can be the answer for "I want something no one else is wearing."
  • Founder-vision authenticity — Tania as a human presence behind the brand is something Mejuri and Kendra Scott's scale prevents them from replicating.
  • Specific aesthetic territory — if Jewels Jar has a defined visual language (intricate, cultural, specific to a design heritage), that's a differentiation that's hard to mass-produce and worth making central to the creative narrative.

§6.4 Shipping reality and how it affects ad strategy constraint

US consumers expect free shipping at a threshold (typically $50–75) and delivery within 5–7 business days. International shipping means longer delivery windows and potentially visible international shipping fees at checkout.

This creates a real conversion problem that ad optimization cannot fix. The options:

  • US fulfillment partner (ShipBob, Amazon FBA, a 3PL) — invest in inventory held in the US. Enables fast shipping. Requires capital investment and logistics setup.
  • Transparent delayed-shipping positioning — Acknowledge shipping time explicitly and frame it as part of the brand story ("ships from our studio — made to last, worth the wait"). Reduces checkout abandonment from surprise but limits the audience willing to wait.
  • Free shipping threshold build-in — Include shipping cost in product pricing so the stated price includes shipping. "Free shipping on all US orders" is a conversion driver even if the price point adjusts to account for it.

Before scaling ad spend significantly: resolve the shipping story. This is the single biggest conversion leak for international DTC brands entering the US market. Great ads pointing to a checkout that surprises buyers with shipping cost will have ROAS ceilings that can't be solved by better targeting or creative.

Key decision gates — navigate these before and during launch:

YES → Pixel is installed and firing events

Proceed to Campaign setup. You can run Conversions objective once you have 50+ purchase events. Until then, use Traffic.

NO → Pixel is not installed yet

Stop. Complete Phase 0 setup first. Running campaigns without Pixel means no attribution, no retargeting audiences, no conversion optimization. It's the equivalent of driving with no gauges.

YES → You have 3+ creative assets ready

Start Phase 1. Test 3 different hooks with equal budget. Read results after 5–7 days before making changes.

NO → You have fewer than 3 creative assets

Brief Ashley before launching campaigns. Testing with 1 creative gives you no comparison data — you won't know if a bad result is the hook, the audience, or the offer.

YES → Shipping story is resolved (fast US shipping or transparent policy)

Proceed with scaling once Phase 1 signals are positive. Your checkout friction is manageable.

NO → Shipping cost/time is unclear or unaddressed on product pages

Fix checkout friction before scaling above $75/day. You can test at low spend, but don't pour budget into a leaky funnel. See §6.4.

§7.2 First $500: what you're buying spend guide

The first $500 is data, not revenue. You are buying answers to:

  • What is our baseline CTR from TikTok cold traffic?
  • What's the site visit-to-add-to-cart rate from TikTok visitors (vs our general site average)?

How to spend the first $500:

§8 Open Questions & Reflections

What's still unresolved — inputs needed to refine to v3

§8.1 Structural unknowns that affect the strategy
Open question Q1 — AOV

What is The Jewels Jar's average order value in USD for US market pricing? This is required to calculate target CPA and break-even ROAS. A $150 AOV at 60% margin means break-even CPA is $90. A $75 AOV at the same margin means break-even CPA is $45 — half the tolerance for ad spend.

Open question Q2 — US fulfillment

Is there a US fulfillment solution in place or planned? What is the current shipping cost and delivery window? This is the single most impactful conversion variable outside of the ad itself. The ad strategy differs materially depending on whether fast US shipping is available.

Open question Q3 — TikTok Pixel status

Is the TikTok Pixel currently installed and firing events on the Jewels Jar Shopify store? If not, Phase 0 setup work needs to happen before any ad spend. If yes, how many purchase events have been recorded to date?

Open question Q4 — Ashley's content cadence

How consistently is Ashley creating TikTok content? Knowing her output rhythm determines whether the "weekly creative velocity" model is feasible yet, or whether we're still in "produce 3–5 assets and test" mode.

Open question Q5 — first-month budget

What's the confirmed US TikTok budget for the first 30 days? Below $1,500 and results will be too noisy to conclude much. At $2,000–$3,000, Phase 1 can generate real signal.

§9 Glossary

Term definitions — hover any underlined term in the document for a quick definition

BOFU

Bottom of Funnel — the purchase-ready stage of the customer journey. Hot retargeting audiences (add-to-cart, checkout abandoners). Highest intent, smallest pool.

CPA

Cost Per Acquisition — total ad spend to acquire one paying customer. Must be below (AOV × gross margin) to be profitable. Your north-star conversion metric.

CPC

Cost Per Click — the amount you pay each time someone clicks on your ad. Derived from CPM ÷ CTR. Lower CPC = more site traffic per dollar.

CPM

Cost Per Mille — cost per 1,000 impressions. The baseline cost to reach your audience. TikTok CPMs in the US jewelry category: approximately $8–15.

CPP

Cost Per Purchase — equivalent to CPA in TikTok's Ads Manager reporting. The cost to generate one completed purchase event from your ads.

CPV

Cost Per View — the cost each time someone watches a meaningful portion of your video (definition varies by platform; TikTok typically counts 6-second views). Useful for benchmarking video-objective campaigns.

CTR

Click-Through Rate — percentage of people who saw the ad and clicked. Good TikTok jewelry CTR: 1.5–3%+. Below 0.8% signals a CTA or product-fit problem.

Hook Rate

The percentage of viewers who watch past the first 2–3 seconds of a video. TikTok's primary signal for whether a creative is worth distributing further. Target: 25%+. Also called thumb-stop rate.

In-Feed Ads

Standard TikTok ads that appear natively in the For You feed, uploaded directly in Ads Manager. They start with zero social proof (no view/like count) but have lower CPMs than Spark Ads.

LTV

Lifetime Value — total revenue expected from a customer over their full relationship with the brand. Higher LTV justifies higher CPA. A repeat jewelry buyer is worth more than a one-time purchase.

MOFU

Middle of Funnel — the consideration stage. Warm audiences: site visitors, video viewers (50%+), TikTok engagers. Creative should be product-forward with offer specificity.

ROAS

Return on Ad Spend — revenue generated per dollar spent on ads. 3x ROAS = $3 revenue per $1 ad spend. Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ gross margin %. Target for scaling: 3–4x.

Smart+

TikTok's AI-powered automated campaign type. Hands targeting, bidding, and audience selection to TikTok's algorithm. Works best after 100+ purchase events and with 5+ creative assets. Defer until you're profitable manually.

Spark Ads

TikTok's format for boosting an existing organic post (yours or a creator's) as a paid ad. Retains the original view/like count. Requires creator authorization (30–365 day window). Outperforms In-Feed Ads on engagement and completion rate.

TikTok Pixel

JavaScript code installed on your website. Fires events (PageView, AddToCart, CompletePayment) back to TikTok. Required for purchase attribution, retargeting audiences, and Conversions campaign optimization. Install via Shopify's TikTok Sales Channel app.

TOFU

Top of Funnel — the awareness stage. Cold audiences who don't know the brand. Creative should be hook-first and entertaining. Objectives: Traffic or Video Views. Budget: 60–70% of total at launch.

UGC

User-Generated Content — content made by real customers or creators, not the brand. Looks authentic, triggers less ad-resistance. 48% higher CTR and 26% lower CPA vs. polished creative in cold prospecting.
