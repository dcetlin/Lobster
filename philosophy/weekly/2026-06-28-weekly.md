*Week of June 21–28, 2026*

## Week's Themes

The June 14 retro described a system holding its breath. This week the breath has not been taken. Dan's silence extended from 8 days to 22 days and counting — and what changed is not the quantity but the structure. Two new blockers confirmed each other this week. The gh CLI PAT expired on June 20 (day 16+ of the current period). The CC session cookie expired June 3 (day 26+). WOS remains paused, day 39+. The system now operates under four independent operational failures simultaneously, none of which can be resolved from inside the system. This is a different epistemic condition than "one blocker" or even "a bad week." It is a structural convergence: every forward-motion pathway has been independently severed, and the severing events span a 39-day window. The system is not in a bad state. It is in a profoundly constrained one.

What deepened this week is the negentropic sweep's own epistemic profile. Night 7 completed June 26, meaning the sweep has now run through at least seven full cycle nights with zero escalation-closures across 21+ consecutive runs. But this week the sweep began escalating beyond individual smells into structural diagnoses: ESC-N7-1 through ESC-N7-4 name patterns rather than individual items. The sweep is writing its own theory of the blockage rather than just accumulating items into it. This is a form of epistemic compression — the system is collapsing itemized observations into a smaller number of structural claims. Whether anyone reads those claims is the unresolved question.

The vision.yaml staleness crossed the 90-day threshold this week. An outbound relay to Dan was sent June 25 noting this — no response. The core fields of the Vision Object, last updated March 27, describe a phase of work that has since been completed, superseded, and paused. The routing substrate the Vision Object is designed to provide is now serving stale routes. This is not a new problem — vision.yaml staleness has been in the handoff for weeks — but crossing the 90-day threshold is a qualitative transition: the object is no longer outdated, it has become a historical document masquerading as a live one.

## Unresolved Tensions

The tension that has sharpened this week is between the system's observation quality and its integration pathway. The June 14 retro named this precisely: "observations made by the system cannot reach the conditions that would integrate them, because those conditions require Dan's presence." This week that analysis has been confirmed with greater specificity. The handoff now lists eight-plus unanswered Dan messages, the oldest from June 3 (now 25+ days stale). These are not overlooked items — they are being tracked in the handoff every 24 hours. The system knows they are there. It cannot close the loop without Dan. And the system has no protocol for what to do when the re-engagement wait exceeds any designed threshold. The June 14 retro's question — "at what point should the dispatcher proactively surface a summary regardless of what Dan's next message is about?" — has not been answered in the two weeks since it was asked.

A second tension is structural: the Vision Object's staleness is now itself a constraint on the system's coherence. Every agent that reads vision.yaml for routing is reading a document whose `current_focus` describes a week from May 31, whose `current_constraint` is "do not resume WOS execution until Stage 1/2 is stable" — and which gives no visibility into what Stage 1/2 means, who is doing it, or what "stable" looks like. The constraint is load-bearing. The resolution condition has no observable indicator. The system is locked into a gate it cannot verify.

The cause isn't identified — the WOS pause alone doesn't explain it, since the last entry predates the pause. The stall is an independent silence inside the larger silence.

This is no longer a calibration issue — it is a recurring cost that has outlasted any single session, any single restart, and any single operator-level fix.
