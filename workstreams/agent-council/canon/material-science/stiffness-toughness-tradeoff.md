## stiffness-toughness-tradeoff

**Committed:** 2026-05-23
**Source:** frontier doc — ergonomics-orient/frontier.md, section 2a (Material Science — Force, Geometry, and Tradeoffs)
**Zone:** material-science

Stiffness and toughness are fundamentally antagonistic material properties: a stiff material resists deformation but fails catastrophically when its resistance limit is exceeded, while a tough material deforms and absorbs energy rather than fracturing. These are not points on a single axis but geometrically dependent — the same material configured differently (fibrous vs. crystalline, layered vs. homogeneous) shifts position on the tradeoff curve. Biological tissues are almost universally tuned toward toughness over stiffness for a specific reason: the world presents dynamic perturbation, not static load, and a system optimized for maximum resistance is brittle under conditions it was not sized for.

The composite structure is the biological answer to this tradeoff: hierarchical fiber-in-matrix arrangements (bone, wood, nacre) stop crack propagation by distributing fracture energy laterally rather than allowing it to travel. The ergonomics of the structure matters more than the material properties of the constituents.
