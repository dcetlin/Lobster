## metabolic-taxonomy

**Committed:** 2026-05-24
**Source:** Dan Cetlin — refined through council deliberation May 2026
**Zone:** systems-ecology

A closed generative system has eight distinct metabolic states (attractors), organized into in-process states, terminals, and threshold events. The taxonomy is structural, not metaphorical — each state has a specific causal role and specific transition conditions.

### States / Metabolic Attractors

**In-process states:**
- **Juice:** Generative potential in motion, not yet resolved. The undifferentiated energy of a system that has begun moving but has not committed to a form.
- **Transmutation:** Juice changing character through resistance registers. An in-process state, not a terminal — the resistance register (whether the load is generative or dissipative) determines which fork transmutation takes.
- **Seed:** Stable origination spec, ready for execution. A seed is not yet juice — it is a resolved form waiting to be activated. Can decay to heat if never executed.

**Terminal states:**
- **Pearl:** Stabilized insight. Becomes canon. A pearl is what transmutation produces when it holds under load.
- **Heat:** Dissipated energy, no residue. What transmutation produces when it breaks under load, and what seeds become when they decay without execution.

**Threshold / event states:**
- **Void:** A threshold recognition event. The perceptual transition where a previously imperceptible thing crosses into conceptual scaffold visibility. Void is not an absence — it is the moment of first recognition.
- **Shit (revealed):** Latent entropy surfaced by sweeps. The sweep is a sensor, not a metabolic engine — it surfaces shit that was already there, it does not produce it.
- **Shit (produced):** Entropy generated by execution itself. Conflict markers, broken migrations, bad decisions — the metabolic byproduct of work that goes wrong in execution.

### Structural Relationships

```
juice → transmutation → [holds under load] → pearl → canon
juice → transmutation → [breaks under load] → shit-produced → heat
juice → [sweeps] → shit-revealed → [addressed] → seed OR heat
juice → [recognition] → void-event → [named] → conceptual scaffold → [seeded] → seed
seed → execution → juice   [cycle closes — seeds re-generate juice]
pearl → canon              [terminal]
heat                       [terminal, no residue]
```

### Key Structural Notes

**The resistance register is the fork determinant.** Transmutation is not intrinsically generative or dissipative — the same energy in motion becomes pearl or shit-produced depending on whether the resistance it meets is in the load path of coherent emergence (generative) or outside it (dissipative). See: `generative-vs-dissipative-resistance` canon entry.

**Sweeps are sensors, not engines.** A sweep that surfaces latent entropy did not produce that entropy — it detected it. Conflating the sensor with the source is a systemic error: it produces misattribution (punishing the sweep mechanism) and obscures actual origin (where the entropy was actually introduced).

**Void-event is three distinct steps, not one.**
1. Perception (void): the thing becomes visible that was not before
2. Naming (cognitive): a concept or word attaches to the perception
3. Seeding (volitional): the named thing becomes an origination spec ready for execution

Collapsing these three into "having an insight" loses the causal structure — specifically, it loses the volitional step, which is where most conceptual work stalls.

**Seed → juice closes the generative cycle.** Seeds are not endpoints. A seed that executes produces juice — generative potential in motion again. The cycle is closed, not linear. Systems that treat seeds as final outputs accumulate seed-debt: potential energy that never re-enters the generative loop.

### Provenance

Refined through council deliberation May 2026. Earlier versions conflated transmutation with a terminal state and did not distinguish shit-revealed from shit-produced. The three-step void decomposition emerged from examining why conceptual work stalls after recognition: the stall is consistently at the seeding step, not the naming step. The cycle-closure property (seed → juice) was added to capture that execution is not consumption of a seed but transformation back into generative potential.

**Connections:**
- `generative-vs-dissipative-resistance`: the resistance register is the fork determinant for transmutation — that entry defines what makes resistance generative vs. dissipative
- `stiffness-toughness-tradeoff`: a material-science instantiation of the same underlying principle — systems tuned for resistance under static load fail under dynamic perturbation; transmutation toward pearl requires dynamic load-path coupling, not maximum rigidity

## Council Cross-references

### Transmutation state → Threshold register (resistance registers lexicon)
The transmutation state's fork condition (holds → pearl/seed vs. breaks → shit-produced) maps to the Threshold register in the resistance registers lexicon. The Threshold register is phase-transforming: it either transmutes under load or fails catastrophically. This is the same fork. The transmutation state IS the Threshold register in the metabolic lens.

See: `canon/resistance-registers-lexicon.md` → Tier 1: Threshold (Phase-Transforming)

### Void state → post-Threshold liminal space
Void follows when the Threshold register fires: the old configuration has ended, the new one hasn't formed. The three-step void decomposition (void-event → scaffold → seed) describes how a system navigates the Threshold register's aftermath.

See: `canon/resistance-registers-lexicon.md` → Tier 1: Threshold (Phase-Transforming)

### Hormetic window characterization (open question, partially answered)
The lexicon asks what characterizes the hormetic window. The metabolic taxonomy's transmutation fork is a structural answer: the hormetic window is the range where Threshold-register load produces transmutation rather than shit-produced. The fork determinant (holding structure integrity at moment of load) is the hormetic window boundary condition.
